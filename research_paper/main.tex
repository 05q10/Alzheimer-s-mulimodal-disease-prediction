\begin{abstract}
Clinical assessment of Alzheimer's disease is challenged by fragmented diagnostic streams, non-random missingness across clinical modalities, and heterogeneous disease progression timelines that static cross-sectional models fail to capture. We present an end-to-end multimodal digital twin framework that models cross-sectional syndromic stage, multi-horizon cognitive trajectories, and dementia conversion risk. Built on \TotalRows{} clinical visits across \TotalSubjects{} subjects from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), the system integrates demographics, bedside neuropsychological batteries, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) assays, APOE $\varepsilon4$ genotype, and ComBat-harmonized FreeSurfer volumetric MRI under subject-stratified splitting, backward-looking temporal joins ($\le 180$ days), and multi-tier routing without statistical imputation. On held-out test visits, the calibrated CatBoost TwinStager achieves a Macro-F1 of \StagerTFourFOne{} (95\% CI: [\StagerTFourFCILo{}, \StagerTFourFCIHi{}]) at Tier T4. Multi-horizon delta-regressors forecast 12-month MMSE decline with a Mean Absolute Error of \MMSEMAEXII{} points, outperforming naive persistence (\MMSEPersistXII{}) and linear slope extrapolation (\MMSESlopeXII{}), while conversion hazard models achieve a 24-month AUROC of \ConvAUCXXIV{} with a positive Brier Skill Score (\ConvBSSXXIV{}). Paired evaluations demonstrate that adding CSF biomarkers to comprehensive cognitive batteries yields no statistically significant staging gain ($\Delta\text{Macro-F1} = \StagerDeltaCSF{}$, $p>0.05$), and volumetric MRI fails to improve 12-month trajectory forecasts ($\Delta\text{MAE} = \ProgDeltaMRIMAE{}$, $p>0.05$). Equipped with an interactive Counterfactual Scenario Lab bounded by empirical plausibility guards and TreeSHAP explainability, this digital twin provides transparent, leakage-free decision support for neurodegenerative disease monitoring.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Alzheimer's disease (AD) represents an escalating global healthcare crisis characterized by progressive cognitive deterioration, neurodegeneration, and irreversible loss of functional independence. Diagnostic and prognostic workflows in routine neurology remain fundamentally fragmented. Clinical evaluations typically capture isolated snapshots across disparate modalities: bedside cognitive examinations (e.g., Mini-Mental State Examination [MMSE], Clinical Dementia Rating [CDR], ADAS-Cog), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) molecular biomarkers ($\text{A}\beta_{42}$, t-tau, p-tau), apolipoprotein E (APOE) genotyping, and structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Crucially, these pathophysiological markers evolve along divergent biological timescales: molecular amyloid and tau alterations emerge decades prior to clinical onset, whereas macroscopic brain atrophy and detectable cognitive decline occur late in the disease cascade.

Single-modality machine learning pipelines cannot adequately reconcile this temporal and biological heterogeneity. Furthermore, existing computational approaches exhibit several critical methodological gaps: visit-level rather than subject-level data partitioning induces subtle data leakage; statistical imputation across longitudinal records leaks future disease state into baseline predictors; the incremental diagnostic utility of invasive, high-cost biomarkers over bedside cognitive tests is rarely evaluated with paired statistical tests when findings are null; and static prognostic calculators provide no mechanisms for clinicians to simulate prospective risk modifications safely within empirical biological bounds.

To overcome these foundational limitations, the paradigm of the \textit{Digital Twin} has recently emerged in computational medicine~\cite{nasem2023digitaltwins}. A medical digital twin maintains an updatable, patient-specific computational representation synthesized from heterogeneous clinical observations, enabling clinicians to assess current disease stage, project prospective progression, and evaluate counterfactual interventions in an interactive sandbox.

In this work, we present an end-to-end multimodal longitudinal digital twin framework for Alzheimer's disease staging and trajectory forecasting, built on \TotalRows{} visits across \TotalSubjects{} subjects from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI)~\cite{petersen2010adni}. The framework addresses observational missingness through a calibrated multi-tier stager (Model A) paired with multi-horizon delta-progression regressors, conversion hazard classifiers (Model B), and a Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) sequence stager for longitudinal history evaluation.

\subsection{Key Contributions}
The primary defensible contributions of this work are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Leakage-Free Multimodal Data Architecture}: A rigorous pipeline that standardizes \TotalRows{} ADNI visits across \TotalSubjects{} unique subjects using subject-stratified splits (\TrainPct{} train, \ValPct{} validation, \TestPct{} test), strictly backward-looking temporal MRI joins ($\le 180$ days offset), and complete exclusion of 16 forward-looking trajectory artifacts, avoiding statistical imputation.
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Multi-Tier Cross-Sectional Stager (Model A)}: A gradient-boosted diagnostic model that dynamically routes patient inference across diagnostic tiers (T1 to T4), achieving a held-out test Macro-F1 of \StagerTFourFOne{} (95\% CI: [\StagerTFourFCILo{}, \StagerTFourFCIHi{}]) with Platt-scaled probabilistic calibration ($\text{ECE} = \StagerECEFinal{}$).
    \item \textbf{Longitudinal Progression, Conversion, and Sequential Modeling (Model B \& BiLSTM)}: Target-specific regressors forecasting numeric decline in MMSE, CDGLOBAL, and ADAS11-Cog across 6, 12, and 24 months. At 12 months, MMSE decline is forecasted with an MAE of \MMSEMAEXII{} points (95\% CI: [\MMSECILoXII{}, \MMSECIHiXII{}]), outperforming persistence (\MMSEPersistXII{}) and slope extrapolation (\MMSESlopeXII{}). Conversion hazard models achieve 24-month AUROC of \ConvAUCXXIV{} [0.756, 0.861] with positive Brier Skill Scores (\ConvBSSXXIV{}). Complementing these tabular point-in-time predictors, a Bidirectional LSTM network evaluates multi-visit sliding windows of patient assessments to classify future syndromic state.
    \item \textbf{Clinical Explainability and Interactive Counterfactual Scenario Lab}: A governance suite featuring CatBoost TreeSHAP attributions, leave-one-modality-out marginal sensitivity analysis, full 43-field input-usage lineage tracking, an empirical 5th--95th percentile plausibility envelope guard, and an interactive simulation sandbox applying literature-grounded lifestyle priors from the Lancet Commission~\cite{livingston2024lancet} alongside explicit reporting of paired null findings.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:related_work}

Computational research on Alzheimer's disease (AD) has progressed along several largely separate lines: multimodal diagnosis, prediction of conversion from mild cognitive impairment (MCI) to dementia, longitudinal and sequence-based forecasting, explainable tree-based modeling, and, more recently, patient-specific digital twins. This section reviews each line, with particular attention to evaluation rigor (data leakage, calibration, external validation) and to whether the incremental value of costly modalities is actually tested. We close by stating the gap addressed by the present framework.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Multimodal Machine Learning for AD Diagnosis}
\label{subsec:rw_multimodal}

Recent evidence consistently favors multimodal over unimodal models. A systematic review of 66 multimodal AI studies spanning MRI, PET, CSF, EEG, clinical, and cognitive data reports that multimodal models often outperform unimodal baselines, with average ADNI diagnostic accuracy of 92.5\% and average MCI-conversion AUROC of 0.922 \cite{jmir2026multimodal}. A scoping review of diagnostic and prognostic multimodal models similarly finds that MCI-to-AD conversion is the most common prediction target and that ADNI dominates the evidence base, yet only 10.1\% of models undergo external validation \cite{xia2025scoping}. Both reviews therefore support the predictor families used in this work (cognitive and functional scales, CSF biomarkers, structural MRI, APOE genotype, and demographics), while exposing persistent weaknesses: heavy reliance on a single cohort, limited external validation, and inconsistent reporting of paired modality ablations, calibration, and leakage-safe longitudinal evaluation.

On the forecasting side, Health Information Science and Systems (2025) \cite{hiss2025forecast} used ADNI-2 3D MRI features together with cognitive, functional, and genetic indicators in LSTM-based models to predict cognition at 6, 12, and 48 months. A multimodal attention-LSTM achieved the lowest error, and SHAP supported interpretation. This work is the closest precedent for our multi-horizon forecasting, but it centers on MRI-based deep sequence models and does not provide a clinician-facing workflow with counterfactual simulation, plausibility constraints, or modality-cost ablations.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Prediction of MCI-to-AD Conversion}
\label{subsec:rw_conversion}

Conversion prediction is the most heavily studied prognostic task. Gradient-boosted models trained on longitudinal ADNI phenotypic data have shown strong discrimination: an XGBoost model on 493 MCI participants predicted conversion over 12--48 months with AUROC of at least 0.857 under subject-level train/test separation \cite{xgb2023longitudinal}, and an XGBoost--recursive feature elimination--SHAP pipeline reached 85\% accuracy for 36-month conversion \cite{grammenos2024xgboost}. Multisource models combining neuropsychological, MRI, and visit-history data improved AUROC from 0.855 at baseline to 0.912 as more visits became available, supporting the premise that repeated assessments carry prognostic information beyond a single snapshot \cite{platero2021multimodal}. Imaging-heavy approaches add amyloid PET and MRI shape features \cite{kim2023petmri}, or combine MRI, FDG-PET, CSF, and APOE in a multimodal Gaussian-process classifier that yields probabilistic conversion estimates and outperforms single-modality models \cite{zhang2012gp}.

These studies establish cognitive scores, atrophy measures, CSF tau and amyloid markers, APOE genotype, and visit history as informative predictors. However, they share several limitations relevant to our work. First, most treat conversion as an isolated binary task, without linking risk to continuous cognitive trajectories or projected future syndromic stage. Second, several depend on advanced PET or MRI shape engineering, or on near-complete multimodal profiles, which are rarely available in routine care, and none provides a tiered fallback for incomplete records. Third, probabilistic outputs are seldom evaluated with skill scores against a reference forecast. Our conversion-hazard models at 6, 12, and 24 months, anchored on both CN and MCI visits, and re-routed through the calibrated stager, target these gaps.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Longitudinal Modeling and Data Leakage}
\label{subsec:rw_leakage}

Longitudinal AD data are especially prone to optimistic evaluation. A critical review of leakage in deep-learning AD classification explains that visit-level or image-level splitting inflates performance, citing evidence that slice-wise splitting can yield 94\% apparent accuracy versus 66\% under subject-wise splitting, and recommends subject-level splitting, temporal separation, nested validation, and external testing \cite{leakage2025dl}. Concrete leakage-aware designs have followed. A longitudinal 2.5D framework for CN/MCI/AD classification enforces a strict subject-level 70/15/15 split so that all visits of a participant remain in one partition \cite{pmc2026longitudinal25d}. A leakage-free benchmark on OASIS-2 identifies three recurring problems, namely CDR-related target leakage, improper longitudinal splitting, and weak temporal modeling, and addresses them with group-aware splitting and exclusion of diagnosis-defining variables \cite{pmc2026leakagefree}. On the modeling side, a Transformer-based deep survival model on longitudinal cognitive and MRI data uses separate heads for CN-to-MCI and MCI-to-AD transitions with patient-level separation \cite{sciopen2025survival}.

These works provide the methodological basis for our four safeguards (subject-stratified partitioning, backward-only MRI joins within 180 days, blacklisting of forward-looking trajectory features, and no statistical imputation). They are, however, either MRI-centered, confined to classification, or methodological critiques; none implements these controls across tabular multimodal staging, delta regression, conversion hazards, and decision support in a single pipeline. Complex survival Transformers are also data-hungry, whereas we empirically compare recurrent models (BiLSTM and a masked GRU) against CatBoost and retain the simpler model when it performs equivalently or better.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Digital Twins for Alzheimer's Disease}
\label{subsec:rw_dt}

The digital-twin paradigm has moved from perspective to early implementation. A perspective in \textit{Alzheimer's \& Dementia} proposed digital twins as clinician-facing decision-support systems integrating risk factors, current presentation, treatment history, progression, and comorbidities, to support diagnosis, monitoring, and care planning \cite{dt2022perspective}. The Fulcrum Brain Health and Neurodegeneration model builds individual twins from clinical and trial data and simulates MCI/dementia risk under lifestyle, drug, and supplement interventions \cite{fulcrum2025}; it is the nearest conceptual predecessor of our Scenario Lab, but relies on literature-curated evidence and Bayesian simulation rather than a leakage-controlled longitudinal pipeline trained on real patient visits. A twin built on EEG-derived digital biomarkers (DADD) predicts CSF biomarker positivity with 88\% accuracy and follow-up conversion with 87\% accuracy \cite{dadd2025}, but depends on EEG rather than routinely available clinical data and does not simulate trajectories. AI-generated digital twins have also been used as synthetic placebo outcomes in an AD trial, reducing estimated sample size by roughly 9--15\% \cite{trialtwins2025}, illustrating counterfactual trajectory forecasting but for trial design rather than clinical monitoring. A systematic scoping review of neurological digital twins concludes that multimodal twins can support early detection, risk stratification, progression prediction, and treatment decisions, while noting that many AD twins remain proof-of-concept with limited prospective validation \cite{bel2026dtreview}.

Collectively, these studies show the promise of digital twins but leave open an integrated system that couples empirical longitudinal ADNI modeling, strict anti-leakage controls, calibrated probabilistic outputs, TreeSHAP explanations, and clinically bounded counterfactual simulation.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Tree-Based Models and Explainability}
\label{subsec:rw_trees}

Gradient-boosted trees remain strong for heterogeneous clinical tabular data. CatBoost \cite{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, with ordered boosting and native handling of categorical and missing values, has been applied to ADNI for CN/MCI/AD classification from routine clinical variables, blood tests, neuropsychological scores, and APOE; MCI was the hardest class, and neuropsychological measures plus APOE improved performance \cite{pmc2026mcicatboost}. This mirrors our observation that MCI recall lags CN recall at intermediate tiers. CatBoost and neural models have also been combined with plasma proteomics for staged detection, reaching AUROC of at least 0.90 for MCI versus CN \cite{qin2024plasma}, reflecting growing interest in less invasive biomarkers that are not yet universally available in memory clinics. For interpretation, SHAP \cite{lundberg2017shap} provides Shapley-value attributions, but explanation alone does not establish clinical validity, calibration, or leakage-free evaluation; we therefore pair TreeSHAP with lineage tracking, plausibility guards, and paired ablations.

In summary, prior studies establish the value of multimodal data, gradient-boosted models, longitudinal visit histories, and explainable AI for AD prediction, and digital-twin research suggests that individualized simulation can support personalized dementia care. Existing approaches, however, tend to be fragmented across diagnostic, prognostic, and simulation tasks; rely on ADNI without external validation; and often do not control for subject-level and temporal leakage. Moreover, the incremental value of expensive modalities such as CSF biomarkers and MRI is frequently assumed rather than tested with paired statistical ablations.

The present work addresses these gaps through an integrated, leakage-safe multimodal digital twin that performs calibrated multi-tier staging, multi-horizon cognitive forecasting, dementia-conversion risk estimation, sequential history modeling, explainable attribution, and empirically bounded counterfactual scenario analysis, while transparently reporting both positive and null modality effects.

\subsection{Modular Lineage and Earlier-Phase Heritage}
\label{sec:method_heritage}
The system architecture represents the synthesis of three progressive research phases, structured transparently in Table~\ref{tab:earlier_phase_heritage} and summarized schematically in Fig.~\ref{fig:earlier_phase_summary}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Phase 1 (Exploratory Multimodal Prototypes)}: Initial investigations evaluated isolated cross-sectional predictors: a 2,149-patient Kaggle lifestyle questionnaire screener (95.6\% accuracy), a 2D axial slice ResNet-34 MRI classifier (99.7\% internal slice accuracy on Kaggle MRI slices), an Optuna-blended fluid biomarker ensemble (63.3\% accuracy on a 3,000-row ADNI CSF subset), and a Vision-LLaMA multimodal OCR pipeline for laboratory blood reports. While effective as standalone exploratory prototypes, these models lacked temporal dynamics, longitudinal tracking, and robustness to clinical missingness.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2 (Recurrent Sequential Modeling)}: To model longitudinal trajectories, sequential deep neural networks (Bidirectional LSTM and GRU) were trained on 3-visit sliding windows ($T \le 3$). A rigorous ablation between naive zero-imputation and explicit binary missingness masking revealed that binary masking improved transition detection (84.03\% accuracy vs. 83.43\% zero-fill on 846 test sequences). However, recurrent networks required rigid sequence padding and lacked fast, exact tree-based interpretability.
    \item \textbf{Phase 3 (Calibrated Hierarchical Digital Twin)}: The final architecture adopts symmetric gradient-boosted decision trees (CatBoost) as the primary cross-sectional stager (Model~A) and multi-horizon trajectory engine (Model~B). CatBoost was selected because it natively handles non-random observational missingness through optimal split routing, guarantees zero data leakage across irregular consultation intervals, executes with ultra-low latency ($< 60$~ms), and supports exact local TreeSHAP attributions. Earlier-phase modules are retained as auxiliary intake and visualization utilities, downweighted to prevent uncalibrated bias.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Step 1: Patient Intake, Scope Validation, and Clinical Lineage}
\label{sec:method_intake}
Every patient consultation begins with the ingestion of up to 43 clinical fields spanning demographics, genetics, bedside screening, functional assessments, neuropsychological batteries, structural imaging, fluid biomarkers, modifiable lifestyle behaviors, and comorbid health conditions.

\input{tables/table_intake_lineage_43.tex}

The lineage and scope mapping for all 43 intake fields are formalized in Table~\ref{tab:intake_lineage_43}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Algorithmic Lineage Governance}: Each field is audited to ensure no input enters model inference without a verified code path. Features that do not improve generalization are classified as \texttt{recorded\_only}. Specifically, patient height, weight, BMI, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure were evaluated for Model~A and Model~B inclusion. Paired cluster-bootstrap testing confirmed that adding vitals yielded no improvement in trajectory forecasting error ($\Delta\text{MAE} = -0.008$, 95\% CI: $[-0.031, 0.016]$, $p = 0.42$) while increasing missingness-induced variance; vitals are therefore preserved strictly for clinical audit logs.
    \item \textbf{Demographic Bias Guardrails}: Patient ethnicity and physician identifier are ingested exclusively for administrative tracking and are strictly forbidden from entering tree splitting nodes (\texttt{FORBIDDEN\_FEATURES}), ensuring predictions remain independent of socioeconomic and racial identifiers.
    \item \textbf{Plausibility Range Validation}: Numerical inputs are subjected to automated physiological range checks upon ingestion: $\text{Age} \in [40, 100]$, $\text{MMSE} \in [0, 30]$, $\text{CDR Global} \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3\}$, $\text{CDRSB} \in [0, 18]$, $\text{ADAS11} \in [0, 70]$, and $\text{FAQ} \in [0, 30]$. Values violating these bounds trigger explicit frontend scope warnings and require clinician confirmation before inference proceeds.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Step 2: Cross-Sectional Syndromic Staging (Model A)}
\label{sec:method_stager}
Model~A classifies each consultation visit into one of three standard syndromic categories: Cognitively Normal (CN), Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), or Alzheimer's Disease (AD). Rather than imputing absent modalities with synthetic artifacts, Model~A implements dynamic multi-tier feature routing across six calibrated diagnostic tiers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tier T1 (Demographics \& Genetics)}: Age, sex, education years, and APOE $\varepsilon4$ risk allele count ($0, 1, 2$).
    \item \textbf{Tier T1b}: Tier T1 augmented with bedside Mini-Mental State Examination (\texttt{MMSE}).
    \item \textbf{Tier T2a}: Tier T1b augmented with Clinical Dementia Rating Global (\texttt{CDGLOBAL}) and Sum of Boxes (\texttt{CDRSB}).
    \item \textbf{Tier T2b (Core Cognitive Battery)}: Tier T2a augmented with ADAS11-Cog total score (\texttt{TOTSCORE}), Functional Activities Questionnaire (\texttt{FAQTOTAL}), and Montreal Cognitive Assessment (\texttt{MOCA}).
    \item \textbf{Tier T3 (+CSF Biomarkers)}: Tier T2b augmented with cerebrospinal fluid amyloid-beta ($\text{A}\beta_{42}$), total tau (t-tau), hyperphosphorylated tau ($\text{p-tau}_{181}$), and assay censorship flags.
    \item \textbf{Tier T4 (+FreeSurfer Volumetric MRI)}: Tier T3 augmented with Combat-harmonized hippocampal volume (\texttt{Left.Hippocampus\_combat}, \texttt{Right.Hippocampus\_combat}) and ventricular enlargement metrics normalized by intracranial volume.
\end{itemize}

The decision logic is formalized in Algorithm~1. Model~A is trained using CatBoost with symmetric tree structures: `iterations=600`, `depth=6`, `learning_rate=0.05`, and `l2_leaf_reg=3.0`. To counter clinical class imbalance and prioritize high-risk detection, asymmetric class weights are enforced:
\begin{equation}
    w_{\text{class}} = [w_{\text{CN}} = 1.0, \; w_{\text{MCI}} = 1.5, \; w_{\text{AD}} = 2.0]
    \label{eq:class_weights}
\end{equation}
penalizing false-negative classifications on MCI and AD consultations. Raw logits $z \in \mathbb{R}^3$ are calibrated using single-parameter temperature scaling:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{p}_k = \frac{\exp(z_k / T)}{\sum_{j \in \{\text{CN}, \text{MCI}, \text{AD}\}} \exp(z_j / T)}, \quad T = 0.8554
    \label{eq:temperature_scaling}
\end{equation}
where scalar $T$ was optimized on the held-out validation set by minimizing multi-class cross-entropy loss over $T \in [0.1, 10.0]$.

\subsection{Step 3: Auxiliary Multimodal Modules (Visual, Textual, Molecular)}
\label{sec:method_multimodal}
In addition to tabular numerical features, the framework integrates specialized auxiliary modalities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ResNet-34 2D MRI Classifier}: Axial T1-weighted structural slices are processed through a 34-layer residual convolutional neural network (ResNet-34) trained on 4-class dementia representations (NonDemented, VeryMildDemented, MildDemented, ModerateDemented). In clinical intake, the predicted visual logit $z_{\text{mri}}$ is integrated into Model~A via conservative late fusion:
    \begin{equation}
        z_{\text{fused}} = (1 - w) \cdot z_{\text{stager}} + w \cdot \left(\frac{z_{\text{mri}}}{T_{\text{mri}}}\right), \quad w = 0.20, \; T_{\text{mri}} = 2.0
        \label{eq:mri_fusion}
    \end{equation}
    The heavy downweighting ($w = 0.20$) and temperature smoothing ($T_{\text{mri}} = 2.0$) ensure that 2D image predictions serve as an auxiliary visual observer without overriding verified clinical cognitive scores.
    \item \textbf{Vision-LLaMA Laboratory Report OCR}: Unstructured paper and PDF laboratory reports (e.g., blood panels, metabolic sheets) are parsed using a fine-tuned multimodal Vision-LLaMA pipeline. Extracted numerical entities are mapped to a strict JSON schema with automated boundary validation. To maintain patient safety, automated OCR extractions require mandatory human clinician verification before populating the digital twin profile.
    \item \textbf{Fluid Biomarker Ensemble}: An Optuna-blended ensemble of Random Forests, Extra Trees, and LightGBM evaluates CSF amyloid-beta and phosphorylated tau assays. The ensemble informs Model~A Tier T3 inference whenever lumbar puncture data is present.
    \item \textbf{Kaggle Symptom Screener}: A 21-feature clinical screener evaluates patient-reported symptoms (e.g., memory complaints, functional assessment, confusion, disorientation) during initial intake triage, providing preliminary pre-test odds.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Step 4: Longitudinal Follow-Up Consultations and State Management}
\label{sec:method_followup}
When a patient returns for follow-up consultations, the digital twin updates the longitudinal consultation trajectory:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Carry-Forward Semantics}: Static demographic attributes (biological sex, education years) and genetic markers (APOE $\varepsilon4$ allele count) automatically carry forward from baseline. In contrast, time-varying cognitive batteries (MMSE, CDR, ADAS11) and fluid biomarkers are updated per visit.
    \item \textbf{Calendar Month Offsets}: Consultation dates are converted to continuous month offsets from baseline:
    \begin{equation}
        m = \text{round}\left(\frac{\text{Date}_{\text{visit}} - \text{Date}_{\text{baseline}}}{30.4375}\right)
        \label{eq:month_offset}
    \end{equation}
    aligning observational records with Model~B's 6, 12, and 24-month forecast horizons.
    \item \textbf{Immutable Snapshot State Management}: Every consultation update is recorded as an immutable JSON snapshot. Clinicians can execute rollbacks, inspect historical visits, and toggle scenario lab overrides without altering empirical ground-truth consultation records.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Step 5: Longitudinal Cognitive Progression Forecasting (Model B)}
\label{sec:method_forecasting}
Longitudinal trajectory forecasting is executed by Model~B, comprising independent gradient-boosted regression ensembles (CatBoost Regressors) trained to forecast multi-target cognitive trajectories across three distinct clinical horizons: $H \in \{6, 12, 24\}$ months.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Forecasting Targets}: Three clinical scales covering distinct impairment axes are modeled simultaneously: Mini-Mental State Examination ($\text{MMSE} \in [0, 30]$), Clinical Dementia Rating Sum of Boxes ($\text{CDRSB} \in [0, 18]$), and ADAS11-Cog total score ($\text{ADAS11} \in [0, 70]$).
    \item \textbf{Strict 10-Feature Anchor Input}: To ensure instant zero-leakage evaluation on first consultation, Model~B consumes **strictly 10 core clinical features**:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 4 Demographics \& Genetics: Patient entry age, biological sex, education years, APOE $\varepsilon4$ count.
        \item 6 Cognitive \& Functional Batteries: Anchor MMSE, CDR Global, CDR Sum of Boxes, ADAS11 total score, Functional Activities Questionnaire (FAQ), and MoCA.
    \end{itemize}
    Model~B does \textbf{not} consume historical slopes or sliding lag features, eliminating requirements for prior visit history and enabling accurate forecasting from the very first clinical intake.
    \item \textbf{Delta Formulation and Plausibility Clamping}: Models predict the progression delta $\hat{\Delta}_H$ rather than raw future scores. The predicted future score is reconstructed and clamped within biological scale limits and empirical training bounds:
    \begin{equation}
        \hat{S}_{t+H} = \text{clamp}\left(S_t + \hat{\Delta}_H(x_t), \; \ell_S, \; u_S\right)
        \label{eq:score_clamp}
    \end{equation}
    where $[\ell_{\text{MMSE}}, u_{\text{MMSE}}] = [0, 30]$, $[\ell_{\text{CDRSB}}, u_{\text{CDRSB}}] = [0, 18]$, and $[\ell_{\text{ADAS}}, u_{\text{ADAS}}] = [0, 70]$.
    \item \textbf{Residual Uncertainty Envelope}: Uncertainty bands are estimated via empirical 90\% residual quantiles derived from the validation set:
    \begin{equation}
        \hat{S}_{t+H} \pm q_{0.90}(H), \quad q_{0.90} = [\pm 1.2 \text{ (6m)}, \; \pm 1.8 \text{ (12m)}, \; \pm 2.4 \text{ (24m)}]
        \label{eq:uncertainty_band}
    \end{equation}
    yielding calibrated 90\% predictive intervals that expand naturally with temporal distance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Step 6: Conversion Risk and Progression Hazard Modeling}
\label{sec:method_conversion}
To alert clinicians to impending syndromic transitions, the digital twin integrates a dual progression hazard mechanism:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Supervised Hazard Classification}: A CatBoost binary classifier trained on progression events ($\text{ever\_converted} == 1$ or worsening diagnostic category at horizon $H$) predicts the calibrated probability of cognitive conversion $P(\text{progression} \mid x_t, H)$.
    \item \textbf{Re-Staging Projected Trajectories}: The multi-target continuous scores forecasted by Model~B ($\hat{S}_{t+H}$) are re-routed through Model~A's staging classifier, generating a projected syndromic probability distribution $\hat{p}_{t+H} \in \Delta^2$ at the future horizon.
    \item \textbf{Monotonic Risk Stratification}: Predicted progression hazard is partitioned into five calibrated risk quintiles (Q1: Lowest to Q5: Imminent Conversion), providing actionable risk stratification.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Step 7: Sequence-Based Staging and Missingness Modeling (Phase 2)}
\label{sec:method_sequence}
During Phase~2 architectural benchmarking, sequential deep recurrent models (Bidirectional LSTM and GRU) were evaluated on 3-visit sliding windows ($T \le 3$) using 5 longitudinal tracking features (\texttt{entry\_age}, \texttt{CDGLOBAL}, \texttt{MMSCORE}, \texttt{TOTSCORE}, \texttt{visit\_month}). Two missing-data strategies were systematically audited:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Zero-Imputation}: Missing consultation variables and padded sequence steps were filled with zeros, evaluated on standard masked dense layers.
    \item \textbf{Binary-Masked Imputation}: An explicit binary indicator mask $m_{t,d} \in \{0, 1\}$ was concatenated to the feature representation ($x_{t,d} \leftarrow [x_{t,d}, m_{t,d}]$) and processed through a Keras \texttt{Masking(mask\_value=0.0)} layer, enabling the recurrent recurrent cells to bypass unobserved steps.
\end{itemize}
Controlled evaluation on identical held-out test sequence windows confirmed that while binary masking improved recurrent accuracy over zero-fill (84.03\% vs. 83.43\%), tabular CatBoost achieved superior discriminative performance (84.63\% accuracy, 84.27\% Macro-F1) with zero sequence padding requirements and native TreeSHAP interpretability.

\subsection{Step 8: Counterfactual Scenario Lab and Trajectory Modulation}
\label{sec:method_scenariolab}
The Counterfactual Scenario Lab enables clinicians to simulate hypothetical what-if interventions (e.g., smoking cessation, hypertension management, lifestyle optimization) and project their impact on disease progression. The simulation executes through a five-step mathematical procedure:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Anchor Extraction}: The patient's verified current consultation record $x_t$ establishes the baseline prognostic anchor.
    \item \textbf{Parameter Overrides}: The clinician specifies hypothetical adjustments to cognitive scores ($\Delta \text{MMSE}, \Delta \text{CDR}$) or modifiable lifestyle factors ($s_i \in [-2, +2]$).
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle Multiplier Computation}: The composite progression modifier $M_{\text{life}}$ is computed from literature-derived relative risks (Table~\ref{tab:lifestyle_factors}) using square-root shrinkage ($\text{RR}^{0.5}$) and hard bounding:
    \begin{equation}
        M_{\text{raw}} = \prod_{i=1}^{10} \left(\text{RR}_i\right)^{0.5 \cdot s_i / s_{i, \max}}, \quad M_{\text{life}} = \text{clamp}(M_{\text{raw}}, \; 0.85, \; 1.35)
        \label{eq:lifestyle_multiplier}
    \end{equation}
    where $s_i > 0$ represents adverse risk factors and $s_i < 0$ represents protective interventions. The square-root shrinkage and hard cap $[0.85, 1.35]$ prevent runaway compounding, restricting hypothetical lifestyle shifts to $\pm 15\%$ to $\pm 35\%$ trajectory slope modulation.
    \item \textbf{Trajectory Slope Modulation}: The simulated continuous forecast modulates the natural history progression delta:
    \begin{equation}
        \hat{\Delta}_{H, \text{sim}} = M_{\text{life}} \cdot \hat{\Delta}_{H, \text{base}}
        \label{eq:slope_mod}
    \end{equation}
    producing accelerated decline under adverse lifestyles ($M_{\text{life}} > 1.0$) or cognitive preservation under protective interventions ($M_{\text{life}} < 1.0$).
    \item \textbf{Counterfactual Re-Staging}: Future simulated scores $\hat{S}_{t+H, \text{sim}}$ are passed through Model~A to re-evaluate the projected stage distribution $\hat{p}_{t+H, \text{sim}}$, displaying the net shift in dementia hazard $\Delta P(\text{AD})$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Step 9: Clinical Explainability and Governance Suite}
\label{sec:method_explain}
To guarantee full transparency, the digital twin provides three independent explainability mechanisms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TreeSHAP Local Attributions}: Model~A integrates TreeSHAP (Tree Shapley Additive Explanations) computed directly across active CatBoost tree paths. For each consultation, TreeSHAP decomposes the model output into additive log-odds feature contributions:
    \begin{equation}
        f(x) = \mathbb{E}[f(x)] + \sum_{i=1}^M \phi_i(x)
        \label{eq:shap_decomposition}
    \end{equation}
    identifying the top clinical drivers that push the patient toward or away from cognitive impairment.
    \item \textbf{Leave-One-Modality-Out (LOO) Sensitivity}: The engine counterfactually re-evaluates the stager while systematically withholding specific diagnostic layers (e.g., without volumetric MRI, without CSF biomarkers, without advanced neuropsychological batteries), reporting the resulting shift in diagnostic confidence $\Delta P(\text{AD})$.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Clinical Narrative}: A rule-based clinical narrative generator synthesizes Model~A stage probabilities, top TreeSHAP drivers, trajectory forecasts, and scenario deltas into a coherent, deterministic clinical summary free of generative hallucination.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Statistical Hygiene}
\label{sec:method_eval_protocol}
Model evaluation adheres to strict statistical hygiene standards:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Patient-Cluster Bootstrap}: Because patients contribute varying numbers of follow-up visits, standard sample-level bootstrapping severely underestimates variance due to intra-patient correlation. All reported 95\% confidence intervals are computed using \textbf{patient-cluster bootstrapping} with $B = 1,000$ resamples (seed = 42). Complete subject trajectories are resampled with replacement, and metrics are recomputed across all constituent visits.
    \item \textbf{Paired Hypothesis Testing}: Paired comparisons between model tiers, forecasting engines, and baselines are conducted via paired permutation tests ($10,000$ permutations) and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Assessment}: Probabilistic calibration is quantified via Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across 10 uniform probability bins and visualized via reliability diagrams. Multi-horizon family-wise error rates are controlled via Bonferroni correction.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cohort Characteristics and Observational Descriptives}
\label{sec:results_cohort}
The observational characteristics of the ADNI study cohort ($N = 21,055$ time-axis consultations across 3,034 subjects) provide the empirical benchmark for our digital twin framework (Tables~\ref{tab:cohort_splits}--\ref{tab:missingness_by_modality}).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Syndromic Distribution}: The cohort exhibits a balanced distribution of Cognitively Normal ($N = 8,425$, 40.0\%) and Mild Cognitive Impairment ($N = 8,363$, 39.7\%) consultations, with Alzheimer's Disease representing 20.3\% ($N = 4,267$).

    \item \textbf{Observational Completeness}: While demographics are complete across all records, invasive CSF lumbar punctures are missing in 83.9\% of consultations, and volumetric MRI is missing in 47.2\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:missingness_by_modality}). This real-world missingness profile directly validates the design of Model~A's dynamic multi-tier routing architecture.
\end{itemize}

The experimental results support four core findings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Routing Resolves Observational Missingness}: By deploying dynamic tier routing (Model~A), the digital twin eliminates the need for synthetic data imputation. Tier T2b (core cognitive battery) recovers balanced syndromic discrimination (Macro-F1 = 0.788) from bedside collapse, while Tier T4 (volumetric MRI) achieves peak discrimination (Macro-F1 = 0.796, ECE = 0.0192).
    \item \textbf{Progression Forecasting Outperforms Baselines}: Model~B multi-target regressors deliver robust 24-month continuous cognitive forecasts (MMSE MAE = 2.31, ADAS11 MAE = 3.12) that significantly outperform naive persistence and linear extrapolation, guarded by calibrated 90\% residual uncertainty envelopes.
    \item \textbf{Tabular Ensembles Match Recurrent Nets Without Imputation Penalties}: On identical sequence windows ($N = 846$), CatBoost matches or slightly exceeds Binary-Masked BiLSTM (84.63\% vs. 84.03\% accuracy) while eliminating rigid sequence padding, executing in $< 60$~ms, and supporting exact TreeSHAP local interpretability.
    \item \textbf{Bounded Counterfactual Simulation Empowers Clinical Decision Support}: The Counterfactual Scenario Lab provides interactive what-if trajectory simulation strictly bounded within literature-informed limits ($[0.85, 1.35]$), establishing a transparent foundation for clinical pair-programming and patient communication.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Clinical Risks}
\label{sec:limitations}

Methodological and clinical limitations are structured in Table~\ref{tab:limitations}.
\input{tables/table11_limitations.tex}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Single-Cohort ADNI Bias}: ADNI participants represent an observational research cohort with higher average educational attainment and lower racial/ethnic diversity than general community populations. External validation on community cohorts is required before claims of generalizability can be established.
    \item \textbf{2D Kaggle MRI Model Disconnect}: The axial slice ResNet34 model was trained on augmented 2D slices from an external Kaggle dataset, achieving an inflated internal slice accuracy (99.7\%) driven by slice-level augmentation. In our ADNI production environment, it is downweighted to a 20\% late fusion with an explicit disclaimer.
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle Modifier Is an External Prior}: ADNI does not collect comprehensive longitudinal lifestyle logs. The lifestyle modifier represents an external literature-derived prior from the Lancet Commission, rather than an empirically fitted statistical parameter.
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic Circularity Caveat}: Syndromic diagnosis (CN vs MCI vs AD) is clinically defined by cognitive and functional scores. Consequently, high accuracy at Tier T2b reflects alignment with clinical diagnostic criteria rather than independent pathophysiological discovery.
    \item \textbf{Regulatory Boundary}: The framework is an academic research demonstrator and is not cleared as Software-as-a-Medical-Device (SaMD). It must not be utilized for autonomous clinical diagnosis or treatment prescription.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Scope}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a multimodal longitudinal digital twin framework for Alzheimer's disease staging, trajectory forecasting, and decision support. By enforcing strict data hygiene—including subject-level stratified partitioning, backward-only imaging joins, blacklisting of forward-looking features, and avoidance of statistical imputation—we demonstrated robust predictive performance without data leakage. Our calibrated multi-tier stager achieves Macro-F1 of \StagerTFourFOne{} at Tier T4, while multi-horizon delta-regressors forecast 12-month MMSE progression with an MAE of \MMSEMAEXII{} points, outperforming persistence baselines. Crucially, transparent paired ablations confirmed that CSF biomarkers add negligible value to cross-sectional staging when complete cognitive batteries are available, and volumetric MRI fails to enhance 12-month trajectory forecasts. Integrated explainability, clinical lineage tracking, and empirical plausibility guards ensure that the system operates with high clinical interpretability.

Future work will expand the framework along three key axes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{External Cohort Validation}: Evaluating frozen models on external datasets such as the Australian Imaging, Biomarker and Lifestyle study (AIBL) and the National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center (NACC).
    \item \textbf{Native 3D Volumetric Neuroimaging Backbone}: Replacing the 2D slice classifier with a native 3D Vision Transformer or 3D ResNet trained directly on volumetric ADNI T1 DICOMs.
    \item \textbf{Prospective Clinical Reader Study}: Conducting human-in-the-loop clinical evaluations with licensed neurologists to measure the impact of digital twin decision support on diagnostic accuracy and clinician efficiency.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Appendix A: Data Inventory and Labels}
\label{app:data_inventory}
The consolidated multimodal dataset integrates five primary clinical and diagnostic modalities from the ADNI registry (Table~\ref{tab:data_sources}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Clinical Diagnosis Records}: Longitudinal consensus diagnoses assigned by ADNI clinical committees at each participant evaluation, providing the ground-truth staging classifications.
    \item \textbf{Bedside Neuropsychological Tests}: Standardized cognitive and functional assessments administered during clinical visits, including the Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR Sum of Boxes and Global score), the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), and the Functional Activities Questionnaire (FAQ).
    \item \textbf{Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) Assays}: Lumbar puncture fluid biomarker concentrations measuring baseline and longitudinal pathophysiology, specifically amyloid-beta ($\text{A}\beta_{42}$), total tau (t-tau), and hyperphosphorylated tau ($\text{p-tau}_{181}$).
    \item \textbf{ComBat-Harmonized FreeSurfer Volumetric MRI}: Structural T1-weighted 3D magnetic resonance imaging scans processed through FreeSurfer automated morphometric pipelines and harmonized across scanner models and magnetic field strengths using the ComBat algorithm. Quantified regions include bilateral hippocampal volumes, ventricular volumes, whole-brain parenchymal volumes, and regional cortical thicknesses.
    \item \textbf{Apolipoprotein E (APOE) Genotyping}: Target genetic assays determining the participant's APOE genotype, encoded numerically as the count of $\varepsilon4$ risk alleles ($0, 1, 2$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{Diagnostic Label and Target Definitions}
The models operate on explicitly defined syndromic and longitudinal progression targets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Syndromic Staging Classes}: A three-class target distinguishing Cognitively Normal (CN), Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), and Alzheimer's Disease (AD), established from standardized clinical criteria and cognitive rating scores.
    \item \textbf{Baseline Diagnosis (\texttt{baseline\_diagnosis})}: The syndromic classification assigned at the participant's initial enrollment visit, utilized as a stratification key during cohort partitioning to prevent diagnostic distribution shift.
    \item \textbf{Dementia Conversion Indicator (\texttt{ever\_converted})}: A subject-level binary flag indicating whether a participant diagnosed as non-demented (CN or MCI) at baseline transitioned to AD at any subsequent recorded visit during longitudinal follow-up.
    \item \textbf{Delta Progression Targets ($\Delta y_{t, h}$)}: Continuous numeric change scores defined as the future score minus the current score ($y_{t+h} - y_t$) over prospective forecast horizons $h \in \{6, 12, 24\}$ months for MMSE, CDGLOBAL, and ADAS11-Cog.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Appendix B: Glossary of Medical and Technical Terms}
\label{app:glossary}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MMSE (Mini-Mental State Examination)}: A 30-point screening examination assessing global cognitive domains including orientation, memory, registration, and attention.
    \item \textbf{CDR / CDGLOBAL / CDRSB (Clinical Dementia Rating)}: A clinical staging instrument evaluating cognitive and functional impairment globally (\texttt{CDGLOBAL}: 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3) or summed across six domain boxes (\texttt{CDRSB}: 0 to 18).
    \item \textbf{ADAS-Cog 11 (Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale--Cognitive Subscale)}: An 11-task cognitive battery evaluating memory, language, and praxis on a 0 to 70 penalty scale, where higher scores indicate greater cognitive dysfunction.
    \item \textbf{FAQ (Functional Activities Questionnaire)}: An informant-rated assessment measuring independence across 10 instrumental activities of daily living on a 0 to 30 scale.
    \item \textbf{MoCA (Montreal Cognitive Assessment)}: A 30-point screening tool designed with high sensitivity for detecting early mild cognitive impairment and executive dysfunction.
    \item \textbf{CSF (Cerebrospinal Fluid)}: Clear liquid surrounding the brain and spinal cord obtained via lumbar puncture to quantify soluble biochemical markers of neuropathology.
    \item \textbf{$\text{A}\beta_{42}$ (Amyloid-Beta 1--42)}: A 42-amino-acid peptide whose decreased concentration in CSF serves as a hallmark of cerebral amyloid plaque accumulation.
    \item \textbf{t-tau (Total Tau)}: A microtubule-associated protein whose elevated presence in CSF reflects general cortical axonal injury and neurodegeneration.
    \item \textbf{p-tau (Hyperphosphorylated Tau)}: Tau protein phosphorylated at threonine 181 ($\text{p-tau}_{181}$), serving as a specific biofluid marker of neurofibrillary tangle pathology.
    \item \textbf{APOE $\varepsilon4$}: The epsilon-4 allele of the Apolipoprotein E gene, representing the strongest genetic risk factor for late-onset sporadic Alzheimer's disease.
    \item \textbf{FreeSurfer}: An automated software suite for processing and segmenting structural 3D T1-weighted neuroimaging scans into volumetric and cortical thickness measures.
    \item \textbf{ComBat Harmonization}: An empirical Bayes location-and-scale adjustment technique removing technical batch effects across different MRI scanner manufacturers and magnetic field strengths.
    \item \textbf{Hippocampal Volume}: Bilateral volume of the medial temporal lobe structure essential for memory formation, normalized by total intracranial volume as a sensitive structural marker of neurodegeneration.
    \item \textbf{MCI (Mild Cognitive Impairment)}: A transitional syndromic stage between normal cognitive aging and dementia, marked by objective cognitive deficits with preserved daily independence.
    \item \textbf{Syndromic Stage}: A categorical clinical classification (CN, MCI, or AD) established from standardized cognitive, functional, and behavioral evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Macro-F1}: The arithmetic mean of class-specific F1 scores, evaluating multi-class classification performance uniformly across balanced and imbalanced diagnostic classes.
    \item \textbf{AUROC}: Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve, quantifying binary discrimination capability across all potential decision thresholds.
    \item \textbf{Brier Skill Score (BSS)}: A calibrated performance metric measuring the relative accuracy of probabilistic forecasts compared against a baseline reference forecast, where positive values denote superiority over baseline.
    \item \textbf{ECE (Expected Calibration Error)}: A metric measuring the weighted absolute difference between model-predicted probabilities and observed empirical accuracy across confidence bins.
    \item \textbf{TreeSHAP}: An exact polynomial-time algorithm computing Shapley additive explanations for tree-based ensemble models, measuring feature marginal contribution to predictions.
    \item \textbf{Cluster Bootstrap}: A non-parametric statistical resampling method that resamples entire participant clusters with replacement to generate confidence intervals robust to repeated-measures longitudinal correlation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Appendix C: Auxiliary and Earlier-Phase Components}
\label{app:auxiliary_components}
In addition to the primary CatBoost stager and regression models, five auxiliary components and sequential networks were developed or benchmarked across the platform (Fig.~\ref{fig:legacy_cm}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bidirectional LSTM Sequence Stager}: A sequential recurrent neural network designed for multi-visit historical assessments. Operating on sliding sequences of up to three visits with five core features (entry age, CDR global score, MMSE score, ADAS-Cog-11 total score, and elapsed visit month), it combines bidirectional recurrent layers ($2 \times 64$ units) with Batch Normalization, Dense(32, ReLU), and a 3-class Softmax classifier (CN, MCI, AD) to model temporal transitions across consultation histories.
    \item \textbf{Kaggle Lifestyle Screener}: Trained on Rabie El Kharoua's Kaggle dataset (2,149 records, 35 features). It achieves 95.6\% test accuracy (\texttt{06\_bootstrap\_results.txt}) but is displayed strictly as an auxiliary symptom screening indicator.
    \item \textbf{Kaggle 2D Slice ResNet34}: FastAI ResNet34 fine-tuned on augmented 2D axial slices, achieving 99.7\% slice accuracy on Kaggle. It is downweighted ($w=0.20$, $T=2.0$) in ADNI late fusion due to slice-level inflation.
    \item \textbf{Biomarker-APOE Ensemble}: Optuna-blended XGBoost, Random Forest, and MLP trained on PCA-transformed ADNI CSF and APOE features. On holdout test data, it achieves 48.1\% accuracy (\texttt{06\_bootstrap\_results.txt}).
    \item \textbf{Vision OCR Extractor}: Groq Vision-LLaMA pipeline for digitizing semi-structured clinical reports, achieving 83.3\% exact match on numerical fields.
\end{enumerate}
